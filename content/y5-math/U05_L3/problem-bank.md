## T1 ★ Add and subtract across zero

One number is negative, or the answer is. Mr. David's own decks teach "jump to zero,
then the rest"; this type drills it with bigger numbers.

**Steps**

1. **Find the start on the number line** — the first number in the sentence.
2. **Jump to zero first** — how far is the start from 0?
3. **Work out the jumps left over** — the number you add or subtract, minus step 2.
4. **Jump the rest, the same way** — keep going past 0.
5. **Write the answer with its sign** — below zero needs a minus.

| # | Question | Start | To zero | Left over | Answer |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 4 − 7 | 4 | 4 | 3 | −3 |
| 2 | −3 + 8 | −3 | 3 | 5 | 5 |
| 3 | 6 − 11 | 6 | 6 | 5 | −5 |
| 4 | −8 + 3 | −8 | — (3 does not reach 0) | — | −5 |
| 5 | 13 − 20 | 13 | 13 | 7 | −7 |
| 6 | −15 + 32 | −15 | 15 | 17 | 17 |
| 7 | 25 − 60 | 25 | 25 | 35 | −35 |
| 8 | −48 + 75 | −48 | 48 | 27 | 27 |
| 9 | 9 − 130 | 9 | 9 | 121 | −121 |
| 10 | −70 + 1240 | −70 | 70 | 1170 | 1170 |

Item 4 is the check that zero is only a bridge when you cross it: −8 + 3 stops at −5.
Expect −13 on item 2 (−3 + 8 treated as "add 3 and 8, keep the minus": the scheme of
work's misconception) and 121 on item 9 (the gap, not the answer).

## T2 ★ Temperatures: rise and fall from a table

The table (made-up numbers, one morning in January):

| Place | Temperature |
| --- | --- |
| Fansipan (top) | −4 °C |
| Sa Pa | 1 °C |
| Hà Nội | 12 °C |
| Tokyo | 3 °C |
| Toronto | −9 °C |
| Helsinki | −13 °C |
| Bangkok | 27 °C |

**Steps** (T5 uses the same five)

1. **Find the start** — read it from the table or the story.
2. **Find the up or down word** — rises / warmer = +, falls / colder = −.
3. **Write the number sentence** — start, sign, change.
4. **Use zero as a bridge** — jump to 0, then the rest.
5. **Write the answer with its unit** — °C, floor, dong.

| # | Question | Start | Word → sign | Number sentence | Answer |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Sa Pa falls by 3 °C. New temperature? | 1 | falls → − | 1 − 3 | −2 °C |
| 2 | Toronto rises by 5 °C. New temperature? | −9 | rises → + | −9 + 5 | −4 °C |
| 3 | Fansipan gets 10 °C warmer. New temperature? | −4 | warmer → + | −4 + 10 | 6 °C |
| 4 | Tokyo gets 8 °C colder. New temperature? | 3 | colder → − | 3 − 8 | −5 °C |
| 5 | Helsinki rises by 20 °C. New temperature? | −13 | rises → + | −13 + 20 | 7 °C |
| 6 | Hà Nội drops by 15 °C. New temperature? | 12 | drops → − | 12 − 15 | −3 °C |
| 7 | Toronto falls by 4 °C and Helsinki rises by 1 °C. Which is colder now? | −9 and −13 | falls → −, rises → + | −9 − 4 and −13 + 1 | Toronto (−13 °C is colder than −12 °C) |
| 8 | What is the difference between Bangkok and Helsinki? | (T3) | — | 13 to 0, then 27 | 40 °C |

## T3 ★ The difference between

A difference is the gap between two numbers. It is never negative. On the slides:
"Subtract 5 from 2" is 2 − 5, but "the difference between 2 and 5" is 3.

**Steps**

1. **Mark both numbers on the line**
2. **Count from the lower number to 0**
3. **Count from 0 to the higher number**
4. **Add the two parts together**
5. **Check: a difference is never negative**

| # | Question | Lower to 0 | 0 to higher | Answer |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | The difference between 2 and 9 | — (no zero to cross: 9 − 2) | — | 7 |
| 2 | The difference between −3 and 5 | 3 | 5 | 8 |
| 3 | The difference between −7 and 1 | 7 | 1 | 8 |
| 4 | The difference between −10 and 10 | 10 | 10 | 20 |
| 5 | How much warmer is 6 °C than −4 °C? | 4 | 6 | 10 °C |
| 6 | How many floors from B2 up to floor 7? | 2 | 7 | 9 floors |
| 7 | The difference between −15 and 25 | 15 | 25 | 40 |
| 8 | Two temperatures are 9 °C apart. One is 4 °C. The other is below zero. What is it? | (work back: 4 to 0 is 4, so 5 more) | — | −5 °C |

Expect 2 on item 2 (5 − 3, the minus sign ignored) and −8 on item 3.

## T5 Words to number sentence: floors and money

Uses T2's five steps. B1 = −1, B2 = −2 (floors below the ground floor, G = 0); money you
owe is negative.

| # | Question | Number sentence | Answer |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | The lift is on floor 2. It goes down 5 floors. Which floor? | 2 − 5 | −3 (B3) |
| 2 | The lift is on B4. It goes up 6 floors. Which floor? | −4 + 6 | 2 |
| 3 | Mr. David parks on B2. His office is on floor 9. How many floors does the lift go up? | gap from −2 to 9 | 11 floors |
| 4 | Mr. David has 10 000 dong. He spends 18 000 dong. He borrows the rest. How much does he owe? | 10 000 − 18 000 | −8 000: he owes 8 000 dong |
| 5 | Mr. David owes 12 000 dong. He pays back 7 000 dong. How much does he owe now? | −12 000 + 7 000 | −5 000: he still owes 5 000 dong |
| 6 | Mr. David owes 6 000 dong. His aunt gives him 20 000 dong. He pays what he owes. How much does he have? | −6 000 + 20 000 | 14 000 dong |
| 7 | Subtract 9 from 3. | 3 − 9 | −6 |
| 8 | A diver is 8 m below the surface (−8 m). She goes 5 m deeper, then swims up 11 m. Where is she now? | −8 − 5 = −13, then −13 + 11 | −2 m (2 m below the surface) |

Expect 9 − 3 = 6 on item 7: the number after *from* comes first.

## T6 Shapes stand for numbers (5Nc.02)

▲, ●, ■ and ★ each stand for a number. In one item, the same shape is the same number.

| # | Question | Answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | ▲ + 5 = 12 | ▲ = 7 |
| 2 | ■ − 8 = 15 | ■ = 23 |
| 3 | ▲ + ● = 20. Write three different pairs. | any three pairs that add to 20, e.g. 1 and 19, 10 and 10, 15 and 5 |
| 4 | ▲ + ● = 10 and ▲ − ● = 2 | ▲ = 6, ● = 4 |
| 5 | ▲ + ● = 15 and ▲ = ● + ● | ● = 5, ▲ = 10 |

## T9 Number puzzles: the same total on every side

A triangle has a circle at each corner and one in the middle of each side (six circles).
A square has eight (four corners, four middles). A corner counts for both of its sides.

| # | Question | Answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Triangle. Use 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 once each. Each side totals 9. | corners 1, 2, 3; middles 6 (between 1 and 2), 4 (between 2 and 3), 5 (between 3 and 1) |
| 2 | Triangle, 1 to 6. Each side totals 12. | corners 4, 5, 6; middles 3 (4–5), 1 (5–6), 2 (6–4) |
| 3 | Triangle, 1 to 6. Each side totals 10. | corners 1, 3, 5; middles 6 (1–3), 2 (3–5), 4 (5–1) |
| 4 | Triangle, 1 to 6. Each side totals 11. | corners 2, 4, 6; middles 5 (2–4), 1 (4–6), 3 (6–2) |
| 5 | Triangle. Use −2, −1, 0, 1, 2, 3 once each. Each side totals 1. | corners −2, 0, 2; middles 3 (−2 and 0), −1 (0 and 2), 1 (2 and −2) |
| 6 | Square. Use 1 to 8 once each. Each side totals 13. | corners 1, 4, 6, 5 in order round the square; middles 8 (1–4), 3 (4–6), 2 (6–5), 7 (5–1) |

Hint for the teacher: the corners are counted twice, so (sum of all numbers + sum of the
corners) = number of sides × side total. Item 1: 21 + corners = 27, so the corners add to 6.
