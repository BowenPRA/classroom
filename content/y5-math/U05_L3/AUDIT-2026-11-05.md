# Clarity pass: Y5 Maths U05_L3 "Below Zero in Words" (5 Nov 2026)

A fresh audit. I did not read the deck's source, plan or builder notes. I judged
only what the class sees: the 24 project-mode screenshots (every answer box open),
plus my own walk of the live deck at 1366×768 in project mode (headless Chrome,
port 9561). In that walk I opened every widget in every state: the lift (all 5
moves, Back, Start again), the strip (all 4 presses plus the clue, Back, Start
again), the Up or Down? game (all 8 rounds, question and answer), every reveal and
every Expand view. My screenshots are in the scratchpad at
`clarity-shots\states\`. Text sizes are the computed font size times the SVG
scale, measured on screen. For level, I checked the Year 5 scheme of work (Stage 5,
5.4 Topic 1, pp. 122–127: 5Nc.01, 5Ni.01) and the Unit 5 end-of-unit test.

All the arithmetic on every slide, reveal and widget state is correct.

## Wrong

1. **Slide 24, "Overnight" (exit question).** "What is the difference?" is answered
   inside the question ("By morning it falls by 12 °C"), and it does not say which
   two numbers to compare. The answer box confirms it: "The difference is 12
   degrees." **Fix:** give two temperatures and ask for the gap, then a rise:
   "At 9 p.m. it is 4 °C. At 6 a.m. it is −5 °C. a What is the difference?
   b Then it rises by 8 °C. What is the temperature now?" (Answers: 9 °C; 3 °C.)

2. **Slide 12, "Mr. David's Homework".** Line a) "4 − 106" is question 4b of the
   Unit 5 end-of-unit test, word for word. The standard asks for original numbers.
   **Fix:** use "5 − 108 = 103" (right answer −103). It keeps the same mistake
   ("he found the gap, not the answer").

3. **Slides 2 and 23, goals vs checklist.** Slide 2 has three goals: temperature,
   floors, gap. Slide 23 has four ticks, adding "Estimate first, then use zero as
   a bridge." Neither slide mentions money owed, but slides 13, 14 (rounds 4 and
   8), 19b, 21 and 22 teach it. The standard says the checklist repeats the three
   goals. **Fix:** change goal 2 to "Go below zero with floors and money." (36
   characters), with the picture showing the lift and a coin. Then give slide 23
   three ticks: (1) a temperature that rises or falls past 0 °C, estimate first,
   zero as a bridge; (2) floors and money below zero, B2 = −2, owe 5 000 = −5 000;
   (3) the difference between two numbers, the gap.

4. **Slide 3, "Mr. David's Lift" (stepper).** NEXT MOVE moves the car to a new
   floor that nobody predicted. Move 1 ends on B3 and move 2 starts on B2. Move 2
   ends on floor 1 and move 3 starts on floor 3. Move 3 ends on B1 and move 4
   starts on B3. The last move also ends on B2 (−2), which is the answer to the
   starter on the very next slide. **Fix:** chain the moves so each starts where
   the last one stopped, and do not end on −2: 2 − 5 = −3, −3 + 8 = 5,
   5 − 7 = −2, −2 + 3 = 1, 1 − 4 = −3.

5. **Slide 5, "Floor −2 or Floor B2?"** In the Dublin photo the lit button is −2.
   In the Beijing photo the lit button is B3, and B1 has a green ring. With the
   big "=" between them, the pictures say −2 = B3. **Fix:** draw a ring on B2 in
   the Beijing photo and on −2 in the Dublin photo, so that the "=" joins those
   two.

6. **Whole deck: no sentence frame for any answer.** The standard asks for a
   sentence frame for the answer. The scheme of work's key phrases for this
   topic are "An estimate for the answer is …" and "The difference between … and
   … is …". The test's question word "Calculate" never appears. **Fix:** on slide
   11 put "An estimate for the answer is ___." under the question. On slide 16
   (or 17) put "The difference between −4 and 5 is ___." Start slide 10 with
   "Calculate."

## Unclear / repeats

1. **Slide 1, starter.** "Car park" is British English. Vietnamese children are
   more likely to know "parking" or "basement". **Fix:** put a small car icon
   beside "car park", or write "to the car park for cars".

2. **Slide 2, "Today You Will…": size and words in the pictures.** The three goal
   lines are 17.8 px. The button labels inside the lift picture (2, 1, G, B1, B2)
   are 11–13 px, and they are words in a picture that should have none.
   **Fix:** make the goal lines 20 px or more (37 characters still fit), and draw
   the buttons as plain circles, with the lowest one yellow.

3. **Slide 2: saying it twice.** The column titles repeat the goal lines ("Under
   the ground" over "Go down to floors under the ground"; "The gap" over "Find the
   gap between two numbers"). The banner adds a fourth line, which uses "number
   sentence" before the lesson has shown one. **Fix:** drop the banner. The three
   goals say it.

4. **Slides 2, 19, 20: "number sentence" is never shown.** Three slides ask for a
   number sentence, but no slide points at one. **Fix:** in slide 4's answer write
   "Number sentence: 4 − 6 = −2."

5. **Slide 3, lift labels.** Above the ground the grey numbers and the blue
   floor names are the same ("5 5", "4 4" … "1 1"), which looks like a mistake.
   They differ only at G/0 and B1–B3. **Fix:** add two small headings, "number"
   and "button", at 20 px or more.

6. **Slide 3, "say it first" three times.** It is in the kicker ("Say it
   first"), in the panel ("Which floor? Say it first.") and in the footer ("Say
   the floor before each press."). **Fix:** cut the footer to "The lift stops at G
   on the way."

7. **Slide 6, "A Cold Night on Fansipan": text too small.** The panel captions
   are 15.8 px (16.5 px in Expand), the thermometer numbers 14.3 px and the clue
   17.9 px. These captions are the story the class reads. **Fix:** captions at 22
   px or more (two short lines fit under each panel), thermometer numbers and clue
   at 20 px or more.

8. **Slide 6: "falls" before it is taught.** Panels 2 and 3 say "It falls by 3
   °C." The next slide (7) is the one that teaches "falls", and "It" has no noun.
   **Fix:** swap slides 6 and 7 so the key words come first, and write "The
   temperature falls by 3 °C." in panel 2.

9. **Slide 6: the answer is never shown.** The strip stops on the frozen
   thermometer and the clue, and no slide shows −4 °C. **Fix:** add a fifth press
   that melts the ice and shows −4 °C.

10. **Slide 7, "Up Words and Down Words".** The line above the panel ("Sa Pa can
    go below 0 °C in winter.") uses none of the up or down words, and it sits above
    the panel rather than under it. **Fix:** replace it with a plain info note
    under the panel: "Sa Pa is 2 °C. The temperature falls by 3 °C. 2 − 3 = −1 °C."

11. **Slide 9, "Zero Is a Bridge".** "Bridge" appears three times: in the title,
    the panel and the diagram label "zero / the bridge" (17.6 px). "Then jump the
    rest" is hard English at A2. **Fix:** delete the diagram label and write the
    panel as "Zero is a bridge. First jump to 0. Then jump again."

12. **Slide 10, "Bigger Jumps".** "Draw a line." could mean any line. The labels
    "subtract: jump left" and "add: jump right" are 19.1 px. **Fix:** "Draw a
    number line like this one. Put 0 in the middle." Make the labels 20 px or
    more.

13. **Slide 13, "Money You Owe".** The same fact is said three times: "He has to
    pay 5 000 dong back.", "He owes 5 000 dong: −5 000" and the note "20 000 −
    25 000 = −5 000". The story never says the seller lets him pay later, so
    nobody is there to "pay back". The jumps are labelled "20 000" and "5 000"
    with no sign, while slide 9 labels its jumps "+3" and "+5". **Fix:** use one
    caption, "He pays 20 000 now. He owes the seller 5 000.", keep the note, and
    label the jumps "−20 000" and "−5 000".

14. **Slide 13: "negative".** The word first appears inside the Owe panel. No
    earlier slide says how to read "−2". This is fine if L1–L2 taught it.
    **Fix:** if not, add "(negative 2)" after "B2 = −2" in slide 5's footer.

15. **Slide 14, "Up or Down?" round 5.** "The diver goes 3 metres deeper." The
    diver is never shown, and "deeper" is explained only after the answer
    ("Deeper means further down."). **Fix:** put a small picture of a diver under
    the water on the question.

16. **Slide 14, round 8 answer.** "He owes less, so he goes up towards 0." Mr.
    David does not go up. His number does. Also, round 8 is the only real trap
    round. **Fix:** "He owes less: −5 000 goes up towards 0." Add a second trap,
    for example "Mr. David pays back 2 000 dong he owes." (up).

17. **Slide 15, "Two Questions, Two Answers".** The tips ("The number after from
    comes first." / "A difference is a gap.") are 17.8 px, and the bottom half of
    each card is empty. **Fix:** make the tips 20 px or more, and use the empty
    space for the two number lines: 2 jumping back 5 to −3, and a bracket from 2
    to 5.

18. **Slide 17, "The Difference Between".** "The gap is 9 steps long." repeats
    the bracket "4 + 5 = 9" just above it. **Fix:** delete the caption.

19. **Slide 18, "One Cold Morning".** "New temperature?" is a clipped form of the
    test's question, "What is the new temperature?". The caption "6 a.m., one
    morning in January" is 17.6 px. "Harbin" will mean nothing to the class.
    **Fix:** write the test's full question, make the caption 20 px or more, and
    write "Harbin (China)" and "Seoul (Korea)".

20. **Slides 18 and 24: units.** Slide 18's answer gives a difference as "26 °C".
    Slide 24's gives one as "12 degrees", a word the deck never uses anywhere
    else. **Fix:** use "°C" on both.

21. **Slide 19, "Story to Number Sentence".** The title asks for a number
    sentence, but each item asks only "Which floor…?" or "How much…?". **Fix:**
    add a first line: "Write the number sentence. Then write the answer."

22. **Slide 20, "Make Up the Story".** The kicker says "Talk to your partner" but
    the prompt says "Write a story". **Fix:** "Tell your partner a story for this
    number sentence. Use a temperature, a lift or money."

23. **Slide 21, "Mr. David Has a Day", part b.** "He parks on B3. Then he looks
    for his car." This reads as if he starts looking straight after parking. The
    answer also mixes "−1" with floor names ("4, then −1, then 1"). **Fix:** "He
    parks on B3 and goes up 7 floors to his office. After work he goes down 5,
    then up 2. Which floor is he on? Is his car there?" Answer: "4, then B1 (−1),
    then floor 1."

24. **Slides 21 and 22: where to work.** Neither slide says whiteboards,
    notebooks or partners. "Freezer" (21a) has no picture. **Fix:** give both
    slides the kicker "Whiteboards" and put a small freezer icon beside 21a.

## Fine as is

- **Slide 1 starter:** one clear question with nothing that gives the answer
  away, and no date.
- **Slide 3 lift:** the sum stays grey with "?" until the press. The stop at G
  and the line "2 to the ground floor, then 3 more" show zero as a bridge. Back
  steps back one state at a time, and all five answers are right.
- **Slide 6 strip:** one panel per press. The thermometers match their labels (8,
  5, 2), and the ice block hides the value. Midnight to 6 a.m. is two drops, so
  there is a real trap, with a fair clue. The hats add up (1 + 4 = 5), and "Pip is
  an otter" names him first. It is deadpan and a little silly.
- **Slides 8 and 16 votes:** the problems are printed big, and each wrong option
  is the real mistake (−11 from subtracting; 1 from 5 − 4). Slides 9 and 17 say
  why the wrong option is wrong.
- **Diagrams are true:** the slide 9 thermometer (start, end and both jumps line
  up), the slide 13 money line (even spacing, arrows the right way), the slide 17
  difference line, and the slide 2 pictures.
- **Level:** every calculation has at most one negative number and crosses zero,
  which is 5Ni.01. Slide 10 is the scheme of work's own activity ("draw a blank
  number line, put 0 in the middle"). Slide 11's −98 + 203 matches its −94 + 104
  example. Slide 12b (−5 + 9 = −14) is the misconception the scheme names. The
  strip and slide 19c count back past zero (5Nc.01). Slides 15 and 18 use the
  test's wording ("Subtract … from …", "the difference between", "rises by",
  "falls by") and a six-place temperature table like test question 3, with
  original numbers.
- **Slide 14 game:** everyone stands, answers come one per press, and the footer
  states the rule.
- **Settings and names:** bánh mì, dong, Hội An, Sa Pa and Fansipan. "Mr. David"
  is written PRA's way everywhere. No student names. The word problems are kind.
- **Notebook count:** slide 23's "5 written panels" matches the five write
  panels (slides 7, 9, 11, 13, 17), with no drawings.
- **Layout:** nothing overflows at 1366×768. Every Expand view opens and closes
  cleanly. Most slide text is 20 px or more. The kickers (12–15 px) and the
  "Write This Down" label (13.7 px) are house chrome shared by every deck, not a
  fault in this one.
- **Slide 5 photos:** real, clear and captioned with what they show. They are
  not Vietnamese. Keep them unless a Vietnamese lift panel with B1/B2 turns up on
  Wikimedia.

**Order:** sensible apart from two things. The strip comes before the key words
it uses, so swap 6 and 7. Step 8 has no slide.

**Copy-down budget:** 5 short write panels and 0 drawings, which is within
budget.

**Mix of moves:** slides 18 and 19 are both whiteboard slides in a row. Make 19 a
pair task (A reads the story, B writes the number sentence, then swap). The
closing word problems should get sillier, but 22 (paying his sister) is calmer
than 21 (freezer, lost car). Swap 21 and 22 so the silliest comes last.

**Teacher-course checks:** the teacher's name is right and no student is named.
The strip uses the shared made-up character. The deck is in English only, with no
language switch. I could not check whether the class profile asks for Vietnamese
twins, because TEACHER-COURSES.md was out of scope for this audit.
