# Year 7 · Homework Packet 11 — 180° Apart

`hw11.tex`: Maths **5.1 A sum of 360°** and **5.2 Intersecting lines**; Science **3.4
Tides**, **3.5 Energy** and **3.6 Changes in energy**. The teacher asked (28 October
2026) for questions that "provide interplay between the subjects", so the packet has
three parts:

- **Part 1, Sections A–B.** The maths, as language: the two steps (add, then subtract
  from 180° or 360°), then a reason after *because*.
- **Part 2, Sections C–E.** The three science lessons, in words.
- **Part 3, Section F.** Maths and science together. This section is why the packet
  exists.

Due **Friday 6 November 2026**, printed on the cover in red.

**Extra Homework 11** (`../hw11x/`, *How High Does It Bounce?*) goes out with it.
It carries the decimals, and it has the book's Question 6 (average depth and tidal
range: not 7.0 m), so that question is not repeated here.

## Section F: where the subjects meet

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **F1 · 180° apart** | The title question. The two tidal bulges sit on one straight line through the Earth, 180° apart. The Earth turns 360° in 24 h, so **15° an hour**: 6 h is a right angle (high tide to low), 12 h is 180° (high tide to high). Then the deck's "about 50 minutes later": the Earth must turn about **12° more** a day (the Moon goes round in about 29 days, 360 ÷ 29), and 1° takes 4 minutes (1 440 ÷ 360), so **48 minutes**. |
| **F2 · In a line, or at right angles?** | Sun–Earth–Moon at full moon (180°, a straight line) and at half moon (90°, perpendicular). The deck's *Line Them Up* model gives Mr Bowen's harbour a **6 m** range in a line and **2 m** at right angles: 4 m bigger, 3 times as big (the HW 10 *how much more* / *how many times* pair again). |
| **F3 · A slice of energy** | A pizza storing 3 600 000 J, so **10 000 J a degree**. 8 slices: 45°, 450 000 J (more than the kettle's 400 000). Two straight cuts with a 40° slice: vertically opposite (40°), a straight line (140°, 140°). The two biggest slices: 280°, 2 800 000 J, **1 400 floors** of stairs at 2 000 J a floor. He takes the lift. |
| **F4 · The wind turbine and the fan** | Angles round a point (3 blades 120°, 5 blades 72°), then two energy-change diagrams that are each other backwards: the turbine kinetic → electrical (3.6's Bac Lieu wind farm), the fan electrical → kinetic and sound. |
| **F5 · Explain it with maths** | Mr Bowen says one Moon means one high tide a day. The student writes him a reply (4–5 sentences, a six-item tick list: tidal force, two bulges, 180° apart, the Earth spins, 12 hours, full sentences). |

There is maths inside the science sections too: the tidal range (C1), joules × a
decimal and *how many times* (D1), and how much lower the ball bounces (E3).

## Nothing untaught

Every fact is on a 5.1, 5.2, 3.4, 3.5 or 3.6 slide: the write notes, the book's
joules (2 000 J a floor, 200 J a metre run, 400 000 J a litre boiled), the 390 times
and the "about half" of the Sun's tidal force, the 6 m / 2 m of *Line Them Up*,
Hanoi's 30 cm. Nothing comes from **3.7** (useful and wasted energy, percentages,
*dissipated*); F3's key notes that a real body turns most of the pizza into heat, for
3.7 to say. No number is lifted from the Workbook or from the decks' practice slides.

## Cut for the ten-page target

The first full build was 13 pages. What came out, in case any of it is wanted back:

- **A1 · 180° or 360°?** — a five-row tick table (straight line, around a point,
  quadrilateral, full turn, triangle). Its (b), *63° + 116° — a straight line?*, moved
  into A2 as (a).
- **B1 · Key words (5.2)** — match vertically opposite, parallel, perpendicular,
  corresponding, alternate and vertex to their meanings. The reasons box in the new B1
  and F2(b) (*parallel or perpendicular?*) still use the words.
- **C1 · Key words (3.4)** — complete the four write notes (tide, tidal range, tidal
  force, earth tide). *Tidal range* is glossed where C1(c) asks for it.
- **D1 · Stores and transfers** — sort the eight words into two boxes. The bike-ride
  clues (now D2) cover all eight, with store or transfer for each.
- **C2(e)** — the book's Question 6 (average depth 4.0 m, range 3.0 m, not 7.0 m). It
  is Extra Homework 11's X3.
- **D3(b)** — *where does the tea's energy go?* E4(a) asks the same thing about a
  coffee.
- **F3(b)** — *is one slice more or less than boiling a litre?* The key says it aloud
  instead.
