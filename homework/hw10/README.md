The French scheme is HW 9's (see `../hw09/README.md`): `\fr` and `\frin` lines that
are empty in the English copy, the English key word kept in blue inside the French,
the English decimal point, *en anglais* wherever the student writes. The French copy
gets more room through `\frl{english}{french}` (bigger boxes, more writing lines),
and two `\iffr\clearpage` lines start Section C and F2 on a new page, so that every
block lands whole (the ones before D3 and Section E came out in the polish: each
left a page two-thirds empty). E2 and F1 rows use `\tallen`, the English row
height, so F1 shares a page with E2. Its spare space falls at the ends of A, C5, D3
and F2.

## Section F is why this packet exists

**F3, "Faster in January"**, is 3.3's exit question (*the Earth is closer to the Sun
in January than in July; when does it move faster?*) answered with NASA's numbers:
147.1 and 152.1 million km, 30.3 and 29.3 km/s. The difference is 1.0 km/s, and the
**mean** is 29.8 km/s, which rounds to **30**: the Earth's speed in the book's table.
So the book's 30 km/s is a mean, rounded, and the decimals show it. (e) quotes the
30, so the student does not have to turn back to F1.

**F1** is the other trap: *how much faster* (subtract) and *how many times as fast*
(divide), side by side on the same pairs of planets. Every division comes out exact
(9.6, 1.6, 2.4, 1.25).

Everything in D–F is checked against the decks: the planet speeds are 3.3's (the
book's Activity table), the football model is the Models deck's ScaleShrink widget
(Sun 22 cm, Earth 2 mm and 24 m away, Moon 0.5 mm, Jupiter 2.3 cm, Neptune 7.8 mm),
and every 3.2 fact in D2 is on a 3.2 slide. Nothing is decimal × decimal.
