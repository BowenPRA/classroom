## The framing is deliberate

As in EX 3–11, nothing on the sheet says *easy*, *basic*, *extra help* or *catch-up*. The
cover states a fact instead: *Every question here is an add or a take away — the angles too.
An angle on a straight line is what is left of 180°.*

Every method is printed as boxes. There are column grids for whole numbers (with a small
dashed box for a carry) and for decimals (with the point printed in every row), and an
**orange box wherever a 0 must be written**. The scaffold fades in each question: (a) is
done, the next ones are printed, and the last is an empty grid where the student places
the numbers. The questions are numbered **X1–X22**, because Homework 12 uses A–D.

## Nothing untaught

It uses column addition and subtraction, the Unit 4 decimal rules (line up the points, an
empty place is a 0, 8 = 8.00), 5.1's 180° on a line, 360° round a point and the two steps,
5.2's vertically opposite angles, and a square mark for 90°. There are no negative numbers,
no protractor and no calculator. HW 12's angles (100°, 145°, 33°, 57°, 124°, the 250° garden)
are not reused.

Built and verified: student copy 8 pages, teacher 11 pages, no errors and no overfull boxes.
Every page of both copies was rasterised and read, and every answer in the key was checked
by hand.
